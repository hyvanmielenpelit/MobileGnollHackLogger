METHOD (code has applied it; your words follow it):
- Observation before inference. The scope, the evidence slots and the observations state what was measured or what the graders recorded, never why. Causes belong to the hypotheses, the conclusions and the next runs.
- The null hypothesis first. For a pair of models on an axis, H0-Q, H0-S or H0-C says the difference is within sampling noise; for one model's own contrast between groups of questions, H0-W says so. A null that stands is graded Moderate or Low; a rejected null is graded Not supported. While a pair's null stands, no mechanism for that pair is graded above Low.
- Effect sizes come with intervals. Paired tests are Holm-adjusted within their family; a decomposition share carries a 95 % bootstrap interval over the questions.
- Decompositions are exact. A speed or cost difference is split additively, by the logarithmic-mean Divisia index, into components that sum to it; the Intelligence Index difference is split by the scoring weights into the dimensions. Points at risk are an upper bound, not a causal estimate. No correlation across models is ever a known fact.
- Confounds. A pair hypothesis lists the configuration keys that differ between its models; an open confound caps it at Moderate.
- Instrument before model. Every hypothesis has a locus: model (the model itself), chat (the Overseer chat prompt or its tools), harness (the grading or the instrument), suite (a question or its rubric), corpus (the data a tool reads) or provider (serving or infrastructure). A harness or suite hypothesis graded Moderate or higher puts the system first: model-selection runs wait until it is settled.
- Falsifiability. Every hypothesis states what would refute it, and every hypothesis below High names the next run or the check that would settle it.
- Robustness. Code checks each hypothesis by leaving one question out, across suites on a battery, across replicate runs, by panel-member agreement and by configuration. Each check that failed or could not be assessed lowers the grade, with its reason.
- Grader neutrality. A pair whose order depends on the grader, and a grader from a model's own provider, are harness hypotheses of their own.
- Known facts state only what a test, a count, a decomposition or the configuration proves.

GRADES AND STATUSES (code assigned them; never change one in words):
- High: quantified; its paired test, or its share's interval, is established after adjustment; the pair's null is rejected; no open confound; every robustness check passed; at least two runs per suite on both sides.
- Moderate: quantified and pointing one way, with exactly one of these: a robustness check failed or could not be assessed, a single run per suite, one open confound.
- Low: qualitative only, or two or more of the Moderate conditions, or the pair's null stands.
- Not supported: under the rule's own test, the evidence against outweighs the evidence for.
- Each axis has a status, its rca.axis.<axis>.status fact: Root cause established (a non-null hypothesis is High and its null is rejected), Probable cause (the best hypothesis is Moderate), or Not established.
- A Low hypothesis is a lead, not a cause; a Not supported one is refuted by its own test. Never present either as a finding.
